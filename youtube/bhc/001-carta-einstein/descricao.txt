In 1939, Albert Einstein signed a letter warning President Franklin D. Roosevelt that a nuclear chain reaction in uranium "would also lead to the construction of bombs." Fifteen years later, he called it the one great mistake of his life.

But the real story is stranger than the textbook version. The letter was actually drafted by refugee physicist Leo Szilárd. It sat for ten weeks while World War II began. Its authors considered asking Charles Lindbergh to deliver it. And when it finally reached the Oval Office, the government's first answer was about $6,000. The push that truly launched the Manhattan Project came two years later, from a British report locked in a Washington safe.
