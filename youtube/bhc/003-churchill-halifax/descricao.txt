In late May 1940, with the British Army falling back on Dunkirk, Britain's War Cabinet met nine times in three days. Foreign Secretary Lord Halifax wanted to explore whether Mussolini could help find out what terms Hitler might offer. Winston Churchill, Prime Minister for barely two weeks, argued that the chances of decent terms were "a thousand to one against."

This video follows May 26–28, 1940 through the War Cabinet minutes (The National Archives, CAB 65/13), the Chiefs of Staff paper "British Strategy in the Near Future," U.S. diplomatic records, and the diaries of the men in the room. It also separates the record from the legend: what Churchill's own 1949 memoir left out, where the famous "long island story" line really comes from (Hugh Dalton's diary), and what the film Darkest Hour invented.

This video is history plus commentary. The narrator's opinions are clearly marked as opinion. Quotes from minutes, diaries and memoirs are labelled as such, and the sources are listed below.
