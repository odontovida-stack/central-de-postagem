On Saturday, October 27, 1962, the twelfth day of the Cuban Missile Crisis, almost everything went wrong. A second, tougher letter from Khrushchev demanded the removal of U.S. Jupiter missiles from Turkey. An American U-2 was shot down over Cuba, killing Maj. Rudolf Anderson Jr. Another U-2 strayed over the Soviet Union. A Soviet submarine carrying a nuclear torpedo was forced to the surface in the Atlantic. And that night, Robert Kennedy met the Soviet ambassador, Anatoly Dobrynin, alone at the Justice Department.

This video follows Black Saturday through the White House recordings (Miller Center transcripts), the Kennedy–Khrushchev letters, Robert Kennedy's own memo, Dobrynin's cable to Moscow, Joint Chiefs notes and the National Security Archive's research on submarine B-59. It also separates the record from the legend: the "Trollope ploy," "eyeball to eyeball," the Arkhipov story, and what the book and the film Thirteen Days left out.

This video is history plus commentary. The narrator's opinions are clearly marked as opinion. Quotes from recordings, letters, cables, memos and later recollections are labelled as such, and the sources are listed below.
