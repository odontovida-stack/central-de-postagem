On December 8, 1903, the Great Aerodrome, the flying machine of Smithsonian Secretary Samuel Langley, built with a $50,000 Army grant, fell into the Potomac River. Nine days later, on December 17, Wilbur and Orville Wright flew at Kitty Hawk, North Carolina: 12 seconds and 120 feet on the first try, 59 seconds and 852 feet on the fourth.

The race was over. The fight wasn't. In 1914, the Smithsonian had Glenn Curtiss, the Wrights' rival in a patent war, rebuild and fly Langley's machine, and then displayed it as the first man-carrying aeroplane "capable of sustained free flight." Orville Wright answered by sending the original 1903 Flyer to the Science Museum in London in 1928. The Smithsonian published a correction in 1942. The Flyer came home after Orville's death, under a 1948 contract with a clause that still surprises people.
