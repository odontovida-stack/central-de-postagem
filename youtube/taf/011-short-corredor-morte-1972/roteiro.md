## Narração

California just moved to empty its death row.

On November fifth, Governor Gavin Newsom said he would commute all 548 death sentences to life without parole. That's more than a quarter of everyone on death row in America.

The Death Penalty Information Center calls it the largest act of mass clemency in U.S. history.

But there's a catch. Under California's constitution, anyone convicted of more than one felony needs the state Supreme Court to sign off. That's more than half of death row.

California has emptied death row before.

February 1972: the state Supreme Court ruled the death penalty cruel or unusual punishment. More than a hundred people were spared.

It didn't last. That same year, voters amended the constitution and brought the death penalty back.

California's last execution was in 2006. The American File.
