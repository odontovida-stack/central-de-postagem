On June 29, 1972, the U.S. Supreme Court decided Furman v. Georgia. By a 5–4 vote, with all nine justices writing separately, it effectively voided 40 state death penalty laws, and 629 people on death rows across America had their sentences commuted. Four years later, in Gregg v. Georgia, the Court approved new laws and the death penalty came back.

This file follows that story from the last execution before the pause (1967) to California's 1972 ruling and the Proposition 17 backlash, the 35 states that rewrote their laws, Gregg in 1976, and the first executions that followed. Then the numbers: the 1986 peak in death sentences, the 1999 peak in executions, and where things stood in 2025 and 2026. It ends where the news is now: California's Nov. 5, 2026 announcement that all 548 of its death sentences would be commuted, and why most of those cases need the state Supreme Court's approval.

The video takes no side on the death penalty. Supporters and opponents are quoted in their own words, and every number has a source below.
