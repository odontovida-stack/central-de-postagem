Congress has formally declared war 11 times. The last time was June 1942. In 1973, after Vietnam, Congress passed the War Powers Resolution over President Nixon's veto: consult Congress, report within 48 hours, and bring troops home after 60 days unless Congress approves. This file follows that 60-day clock from 1973 to the Iran war of 2026.

You'll see the two clauses of the Constitution that split the war power, the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution (416–0 and 88–2), Nixon's veto and the November 7, 1973 override, and the wording presidents use in their reports. Presidents sent Congress 168 reports under the law through 2017, and only one, President Ford's in May 1975 (the Mayaguez), cited the section that starts the clock. Then come the tests: INS v. Chadha (1983), Lebanon (1983), Kosovo and the 213–213 House tie (1999), Libya and the meaning of "hostilities" (2011), the 2001 AUMF, the vetoed Yemen and Iran resolutions (2019, 2020), the repeal of the Iraq authorizations (2025), and the Senate's 48–51 vote on Iran on November 19, 2026.
