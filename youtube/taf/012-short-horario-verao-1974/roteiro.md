## Narração

This month, most of America turned its clocks back an hour. Again.

In July, the House voted 308 to 117 to make daylight saving time permanent. It still isn't law.

America already tried that once.

December 1973. In the middle of the Arab oil embargo, President Nixon signed year-round daylight saving time to save energy. It started January 6, 1974.

At first, 79 percent of Americans supported it.

Then winter hit. In parts of the country, the sun didn't rise until around 9 a.m. Kids walked to school in the dark.

By February, support had dropped to 42 percent.

That October, President Ford signed a law bringing standard time back for the winter.

The experiment was supposed to last more than a year. It lasted less than ten months.
