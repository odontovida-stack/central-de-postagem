## Narração

New international students at American colleges fell about eighteen percent last school year. That's the second drop in a row.

Early numbers for this fall show new students down about fifteen percent more.

Nearly nine in ten colleges surveyed said the visa process hurt their enrollment.

America still hosts more than a million international students. And it has seen a slide like this before.

In November 2004, a national report found that foreign enrollment in the U.S. had fallen for the first time in more than thirty years.

The drop was small, about two percent. But the reasons sound familiar: student visa delays after September eleventh, rising tuition, and countries like Britain, Canada and Australia recruiting harder.

Back then, America hosted about five hundred seventy thousand international students. Today it's about twice that.
