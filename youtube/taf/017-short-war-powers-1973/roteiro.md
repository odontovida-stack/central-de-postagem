## Narração

This week, the Senate voted on a resolution directing the president to pull U.S. forces out of military operations against Iran.

It failed, 48 to 51. Senators crossed party lines in both directions.

That vote was possible because of a law passed more than fifty years ago.

In 1973, as America left Vietnam, Congress passed the War Powers Resolution. A president must notify Congress within 48 hours of sending troops into hostilities, and end that use of force within 60 days unless Congress approves it.

President Nixon called the law unconstitutional and vetoed it.

On November 7, 1973, Congress overrode him: 284 to 135 in the House, 75 to 18 in the Senate.

Half a century later, the fight over who decides on war is still being voted on.
