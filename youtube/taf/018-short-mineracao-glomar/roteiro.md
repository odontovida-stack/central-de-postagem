## Narração

This week, the U.S. held its first-ever auction of deep-sea mining leases: more than thirty million acres of seabed off American Samoa.

The prize: metal-rich nodules nearly four miles down, holding nickel, cobalt, copper and manganese.

The sale went ahead days after a federal judge ruled that a key wildlife review behind it violated the Endangered Species Act. Lawsuits continue.

But America's most famous deep-sea mining ship never mined at all.

In 1974, the Hughes Glomar Explorer said it was harvesting manganese nodules from the Pacific floor. Its real mission, for the CIA: lift a sunken Soviet nuclear-missile submarine from more than three miles down.

It recovered only part of it. When reporters exposed the story in 1975, the government would neither confirm nor deny it.

That answer is still called the Glomar response.
