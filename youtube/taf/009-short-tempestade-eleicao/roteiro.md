# Short avulso 009 — When storms hit Election Day: Florida 2026 vs. Sandy 2012

Ver short.json (narração) e slides.json. Sem vídeo longo. Pauta semanal "notícia da semana + paralelo histórico". Fatos conferidos em 07/11/2026.

## Notícia
No dia da eleição (3/11/2026), chuva e enchentes obrigaram a Flórida a mudar 21 locais de votação; eleitores avisados por SMS, e-mail e cartazes (AP).

## Paralelo histórico
Furacão Sandy (2012): tocou a terra em Nova Jersey em 29/10, 8 dias antes da eleição presidencial; NJ estendeu o voto por e-mail/fax (normalmente para eleitores no exterior/militares) aos desalojados; Nova York permitiu voto provisório (affidavit) em qualquer seção.

## Narração (125 palavras)

On Election Day this year, rain and flooding forced Florida to move more than twenty polling places. Voters got the new locations by text, by email and on signs.

It's not the first time the weather crashed an election.

October 2012: Superstorm Sandy slammed into New Jersey about a week before the presidential election.

Homes flooded, power went out, and many residents were forced to leave.

So New Jersey did something unusual. It let voters displaced by the storm vote by email or fax, treating them like overseas voters.

New York let voters cast an affidavit ballot for president and statewide offices at any polling site in the state.

Election Day is fixed by federal law. The weather never got the memo. The American File.
